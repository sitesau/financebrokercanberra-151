Mortgage Broker Canberra Glossary Explained

When engaging with a mortgage broker in Canberra, the initial conversation should focus on preparation rather than immediate application. It's crucial to understand that a broker cannot guarantee borrowing power, approval, or settlement timing. Regulated credit activities must be handled by a licensed broker or authorised representative. A productive first meeting involves presenting factual information that a lender would assess, such as your income, expenses, savings history, deposit status, the type of property you're interested in, and your settlement timeline. The goal is to make your loan inquiry specific enough for the broker to compare lender policies and identify any missing information.

For first-time buyers, questions should centre on the documents needed to verify savings, explore deposit options, and clarify any concession requirements. Refinancers should bring details of their current loan and the reasons for seeking a review, like repayment pressures or a desire to compare options. Investors need to understand how rental income, financial buffers, loan structures, and lender policies will be evaluated. Those looking to build should separate queries about knockdown-rebuilds, house-and-land packages, or staged payments from standard purchase inquiries.

Key Questions Before Choosing a Loan Pathway

To move beyond a general interest rate comparison, it's helpful to structure your loan inquiry. The guide outlines common loan goals, including first-home buying, refinancing, investment properties, construction loans, self-employed assessments, and bridging finance. Each pathway requires different documentation and involves unique trade-offs.

Consider these questions to guide your preparation:

  • What is your primary loan goal: purchase, refinance, investment, construction, self-employed assessment, or bridging?
  • What evidence will lenders expect regarding your income, expenses, savings, and the specific property?
  • Does your situation involve ACT concessions, and have you confirmed eligibility with the ACT Revenue Office or a conveyancer?
  • Are there property-specific factors, like apartment living or staged building payments, that might prompt additional lender scrutiny?
  • How do you distinguish between advice that a broker can provide and information requiring confirmation from legal, tax, or government bodies?

For general home loan education, ASIC's Moneysmart website is a valuable resource. Remember that individual borrowing power and loan approval ultimately depend on the specific lender's assessment and the property's details.

Deposit, Concessions, and Evidence

Many buyers consider deposit options of 5%, 10%, or 20%. However, the required deposit amount is influenced by the property price, lender policies, your savings history, and whether any guarantees or concessions apply. This framing helps understand that deposit percentages are not rigid rules.

For ACT buyers seeking assistance, a broker can help identify the necessary documents for lenders. However, confirming eligibility for concessions should be done through the ACT Revenue Office or a conveyancer. The ACT Revenue Office guidance indicates that from July 1, 2026, the Home Buyer Concession Scheme will no longer have an income threshold. It is always advisable to check the most current information before finalising any agreements.

It is important to keep lender evidence, concession evidence, and contract or conveyancing requirements separate. Mixing these can create a false sense of certainty during loan discussions. While a broker can assist with the lending aspects, decisions regarding concessions are typically made by the relevant government authority or adviser.

Loan Goal Variations and Paperwork

When your loan goal changes, so does the required paperwork. A refinance review typically begins with your current loan details, the reasons for exploring new options, and your current financial standing (income and expenses). The guide describes refinance support as a structured review of available options, not a guarantee that switching loans will be financially advantageous.

Investment lending is as much about understanding lender policy as it is about interest rates. For Canberra investors, key considerations include how proposed rental income will be assessed, which expenses must be disclosed, and the specific loan structures and lender policies that apply. Preparing for this involves understanding how the projected rental income will be factored into the lender's assessment and what expenses need to be declared.

  1. Organise Financial Documents. Gather evidence of your income (payslips, tax returns), details of your expenses, and statements showing your savings history and deposit amount.
  2. Define Your Loan Goal. Clarify whether you are buying a first home, refinancing, investing, building, need a self-employed assessment, or require bridging finance.
  3. Identify Property Details. Know the type of property you intend to purchase (e.g., apartment, house and land) and its location, as this can affect lender requirements.
  4. Understand Concessions and Timing. Research any applicable government concessions, like the ACT Home Buyer Concession Scheme, and determine your desired settlement timeline.
  5. Prepare Specific Questions. Formulate clear questions for your broker based on your financial situation, loan goal, and property details, focusing on lender policy and required documentation.

Common questions

What is the main purpose of preparing before speaking to a mortgage broker? Preparation ensures you have organised financial information, specific loan goals, and clear questions, enabling the broker to provide more targeted advice and compare lender policies effectively.

When does the ACT Home Buyer Concession Scheme change take effect? According to ACT Revenue Office guidance, the income threshold for the Home Buyer Concession Scheme is removed from 1 July 2026.

Can a mortgage broker guarantee loan approval? No, a mortgage broker cannot guarantee borrowing power, loan approval, or settlement timing. These outcomes are determined by the lender's assessment based on your application and the property details.
